Research

Research Overview

My research interests lie at the intersection of health economics, healthcare policy, and applied statistics. I am particularly interested in using quantitative methods to examine healthcare ownership, organizational changes, and healthcare outcomes.

My current research focuses on healthcare private equity acquisitions and the application of causal inference methods to observational healthcare data. My broader interests include healthcare accessibility, evidence-based policymaking, and quantitative evaluation of healthcare systems.

Previous Research Experience

Accessibility and Human-Computer Interaction @ CoLiberation Lab, Purdue University West Lafayette, IN (Hybrid) Aug 2024 – Dec 2024

This work addressed the difficulty of reading complex web content, especially for users who benefit from clearer structure and simpler language. The objective was to support accessible information retrieval by summarizing web pages into a more scannable brief.

I designed a Chrome-based accessibility extension that uses large language models to simplify complex web syntax, and implemented asynchronous API handling to manage latency between the browser client and the inference server. The project was intended as a technical foundation for later work on neurodiversity and reading comprehension; it did not include a completed user study.

  • Research problem: dense web content that is difficult to parse and navigate.
  • Approach: LLM-assisted summarization with structured, accessibility-oriented output.
  • Deliverable: a Chrome extension prototype and the related Accessibility Web Summarizer project write-up.
Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) Large Language Models (LLMs) Web Accessibility Text Summarization Accessible Information Retrieval
Applied Statistical Research — Beck's Hybrids @ The Data Mine, Purdue University West Lafayette, IN Aug 2023 – May 2024

This Corporate Partners project studied drivers of seed quality and germination using field records and laboratory results from Beck's Hybrids. The research question was which observable production factors were associated with germination and related quality outcomes.

I helped build Python and SQL pipelines to clean and merge large environmental sensor and production datasets, then used multivariate regression and ANOVA to evaluate seed viability, including variables such as defoliation and moisture. Findings were presented at Purdue’s Corporate Partners symposium.

  • Data preparation: cleaning, joining, and organizing field and lab datasets for analysis.
  • Methods: exploratory data analysis, regression, ANOVA, and statistical inference.
  • Deliverable: symposium presentation and poster. Project page · Symposium PDF.
Regression Analysis Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) Exploratory Data Analysis Statistical Inference Data Preparation Research Communication
